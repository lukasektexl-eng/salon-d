# Anti-slop checklist

"AI slop" design means the statistical-average website: what you get when every choice falls back to a default. Owners can't name it, but they feel "template", and a pitch dies there. These are heuristics, not laws. A tell is a reason to ask "did someone *decide* this for this business?"

Go through each line against your page. If you keep a pattern, you should be able to say why it fits this particular business.

## Colour
- **Indigo or violet primary, purple-to-blue gradients.** These are the strongest tell. Take the colour from the logo or the premises instead.
- **Gradient text on headings or prices.** Use solid colour.
- **Evenly spread colour with no hierarchy.** Use one dominant neutral, one ink and one brand accent. Give the accent a job: links, the active state, one signature mark.
- **Invented "luxury" palettes** (plum + gold, cream + terracotta) that the business doesn't own. Derive the palette from its interior, materials and logo.
- **Pure #000 or #fff.** Use slightly tinted off-black and off-white.
- **Cream + big serif + airy "editorial" styling.** This is fine for beauty, hospitality and fashion, but only when the rest is specific. On a garage or IT firm it's slop.

## Typography
- **One word in every headline in italic and accent colour.** Remove it. Headlines should carry meaning, not ornament.
- **Tracked uppercase "eyebrow" labels with a little line before them on every section.** Remove them, or keep them only where they're part of the brand (e.g. a logo tagline).
- **Display fonts that break the language's diacritics.** Render a test line before choosing.
- **Vague slogans** ("Time just for you", "Services you'll love"). Replace them with specific statements: what, where, for whom.

## Layout and components
- **Identical card grids** (icon in a rounded square + title + text, repeated). Use a menu or list, varied sizes, or real photos.
- **Cards around everything; cards in cards.** Use the card test: if removing the border, shadow and background doesn't hurt, remove the card. Keep containers only for interactive things (booking widget).
- **Every container with a big radius and the same soft shadow.** Pick one small radius system (2–4 px works for premium) and use hairlines instead of shadows.
- **Glassmorphism, blurred translucent panels.** Use solid surfaces.
- **Generic hero:** a centred or left heading, a subtitle, two equal pill buttons and a blob. Use a specific headline, real photos and one primary action plus one text link.
- **Two equal-weight CTAs everywhere.** Keep one primary; the rest are secondary or text.
- **Repeated identical section structure** (heading + paragraph + button, ×6). Vary composition per section.
- **Masonry or grids with holes.** Order items so the grid fills, and check both collapsed and expanded states.

## Decoration and motion
- **Gradient blobs, orbs, floating shapes.** Remove them, or replace them with a real photo or a brand gesture.
- **Pulsing dots, shine sweeps, 3D tilt, rotating icons on hover, hover-lift on every card, the same image zoom everywhere.** Remove these. Keep one or two purposeful motions, e.g. content fading in and a brand line drawing itself.
- **Emoji or stock icon sets as decoration.** Use no icons unless they carry meaning; text labels are fine.
- **Weak CSS imitations of real objects** (fake 3D voucher with gradient foil). Do a flat, believable paper object instead.

## Copy and facts
- **Invented badges** ("most popular", "No. 1", "certified"). Delete them unless the business says it.
- **Health or results claims** not made by the business. Delete them.
- **Over-written copy.** Cut about 30%.
- **Placeholders left unmarked.** Mark them clearly and list them for the user.
- **Alt text like "smiling woman".** Describe what's really in the photo.

## The two tests
1. **Identity:** cover the logo. Could this be any other business's site? Then it's not done.
2. **Decision:** for each visual choice, can you name the fact about this business that caused it?
